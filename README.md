## 1. Purpose

This agreement describes how station and livestock data provided by the grazier or grazing operation will be collected, stored, analysed, used, shared, and protected for research purposes.

The purpose of the research is to improve knowledge related to pastoral systems, livestock production, rangeland management, animal nutrition and health, farm economics, environmental sustainability, and data science applied to grazing enterprises.

The research team will use the data only for purposes described in this agreement and will take reasonable measures to protect the privacy and confidentiality of the grazier and the station.

---

## 2. Data Covered by This Agreement

The grazier may provide one or more of the following types of data:

- Station or property identification information
- Animal identification and demographic records
- Species and breed composition
- Herd or flock size and stocking rate records
- Live-weight gain and body condition score data
- Reproduction records (calving, lambing, or kidding rates; weaning rates; mortality records)
- Milk, meat, fibre, or other production data
- Feed intake, supplementary feeding, and pasture utilisation records
- Pasture species composition and feed-on-offer (FOO) estimates
- Feed ingredients, diet composition, and nutrient supplementation records
- Animal health and treatment records (aggregated)
- Parasite management and vaccination records
- Mustering, weaning, and husbandry management records
- Housing, water point, and infrastructure information
- Environmental data, including rainfall, temperature, humidity, and other production conditions
- Soil health, ground cover, and biodiversity survey data
- Greenhouse gas emission estimates (enteric methane, nitrous oxide)
- Sensor, equipment, or automated monitoring data
- Economic or production-efficiency data, when specifically agreed
- Other research-related information: [Describe]

Only data necessary for the approved research objectives will be collected and used.

## 3. Data Ownership and Permission to Use

The grazier retains ownership of the original station data unless otherwise specified in a separate written agreement.

By entering into this agreement, the grazier grants the research team permission to use the agreed data for:

- Scientific research and analysis
- Statistical modelling
- Development and evaluation of predictive models
- Machine learning and artificial intelligence research, when applicable
- Comparison or integration with other approved research datasets
- Academic publications
- Scientific presentations
- Research reports
- Development of research methods, tools, or analytical frameworks

The data will not be sold to third parties.

Commercial use, licensing, or use outside the agreed research scope will require additional written permission unless otherwise specified in this agreement.

## 5. Privacy and Confidentiality

All personal and station-level data is processed in strict compliance with the **UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR)**, the **Data Protection Act 2018**, and — where applicable — the **EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU GDPR 2016/679)**. For international collaborators, the equivalent national data protection legislation of the participating country applies.

The research team will take reasonable measures to protect the identity of the grazier and station. Whenever possible:

- Grazier names and station names will be removed from analytical datasets.
- Personal contact information will be stored separately from research data.
- Station addresses and precise geographic information will not be disclosed unless necessary for the research and specifically approved.
- Research datasets will use coded or de-identified station identifiers (**StationID**).
- Results will normally be reported in aggregated or de-identified form using k-anonymity techniques (k ≥ 5).
- Individual stations will not be identified in publications, presentations, or reports without written permission.

Access to identifiable station information will be limited to authorised members of the research team.

### 5.1 Lawful Basis for Processing

Processing rests on two primary lawful bases:

1. **Explicit consent** freely given by the grazier upon signing this Agreement.
2. **Legitimate interests** of the Research Institution in conducting scientific research in the public interest, where these interests do not override the fundamental rights and freedoms of the grazier.

Any change to the lawful basis requires notification and, where necessary, renewed consent.

Changes to the above infrastructure are notified to graziers in accordance with the amendment process in Section 14.

### 6.2 Data Architecture

Data flows through a three-layer architecture:

| Layer | Contents | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Bronze** (raw) | Data as received from graziers, minimal transformation, full audit trail preserved | Data engineers only |
| **Silver** (cleansed) | Validated, deduplicated, pseudonymised data. StationID replaces all personal and property identifiers | Authorised researchers (role-based access) |
| **Gold** (curated) | Aggregated, anonymised datasets ready for modelling, publication, or grazier-facing dashboards | Programme-wide; published openly where appropriate |

### 6.3 Security Safeguards

The research team will use reasonable safeguards including:

- Data in transit: encrypted via **TLS 1.3**
- Data at rest: **AES-256 encryption** on cloud storage
- Access control: **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** with mandatory Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Secure backups and controlled data-transfer procedures
- Separation of identifying information from analytical data
- Annual penetration testing by an independent security firm

If a data security incident occurs that may affect confidential station information, the research team will notify the relevant supervisory authority within **72 hours** and will contact affected graziers within **5 working days**.

## 7. FAIR Data Principles

The research team will manage research data according to the FAIR principles (Wilkinson et al., 2016), embedded in major research funding requirements including those of UK Research & Innovation (UKRI) and the European Research Council (ERC).

FAIR means that data should be **Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable**.

FAIR data management does not require confidential station data to be publicly available. The guiding principle for this Programme is: *"as open as possible, as closed as necessary."*

### 7.1 Findable

To improve the organisation and traceability of the data:

- Every published dataset is assigned a **globally unique persistent identifier**, implemented as a **Digital Object Identifier (DOI)** minted through a recognised DOI registry (e.g., DataCite).
- All datasets carry structured metadata conforming to the **Dublin Core Metadata Initiative (DCMI)** standard, supplemented by the **AGROVOC Controlled Vocabulary** maintained by FAO. Metadata records are machine-readable in JSON-LD.
- An internal data catalogue (**Databricks Unity Catalog**) records all datasets by StationID category, collection date, data type, and research use.
- Published datasets are indexed by **OpenAIRE** and **Google Dataset Search**.
- Dataset versions and major data-processing steps are recorded.

These practices help researchers locate, understand, and manage the data correctly.

### 7.2 Accessible

Access to station data depends on the sensitivity of the information and the permissions granted under this agreement.

- Identifiable station data will not automatically be made publicly available.
- Published anonymised datasets are accessible via **HTTPS** through repository APIs, with no authentication required.
- Where datasets require restricted access, the **metadata record remains open** and access can be requested through a documented process. Researchers must complete an access form, agree to a Data Access Agreement, and receive approval from the Programme Data Access Committee within **20 working days**. Access is granted via time-limited, audited API tokens.
- The Programme commits to maintaining dataset DOI resolution and metadata accessibility for a minimum of **10 years** beyond the Programme's end date, through deposit with a long-term preservation repository (e.g., UK Data Archive).

### 7.3 Interoperable

Whenever practical, data will be organised using commonly accepted formats and standards.

**Open file formats used:**

| Data Type | Format |
|---|---|
| Tabular data | CSV, Parquet |
| Geospatial data | GeoJSON, GeoTIFF |
| Time-series sensor data | NetCDF |
| Metadata | JSON-LD, Dublin Core XML |

**Ontologies used for variable annotation:**

| Ontology | Purpose |
|---|---|
| AGRO (Agronomy Ontology) | Grazing and pasture management practice terms |
| LBO (Livestock Breed Ontology) | Standardised breed classification across cattle, sheep, and goats |
| ENVO (Environment Ontology) | Rangeland, pasture, and habitat environment terms |
| OBI (Ontology for Biomedical Investigations) | Animal health and production trial terms |
| CHEBI (Chemical Entities of Biological Interest) | Veterinary treatment and supplement compounds |

The Programme's internal data platform exposes data through a **REST API** conforming to the **OpenAPI 3.1** specification. Data exchange with collaborating institutions uses the **W3C PROV-O** provenance ontology. All geospatial data is published in **EPSG:4326 (WGS84)**; transformations to British National Grid (EPSG:27700) or GDA2020 (EPSG:7844) are available on request.

### 7.4 Reusable

To support responsible future use and reproducibility:

- Licence terms are clearly stated (see Section 4) and are machine-readable and embedded in each dataset's metadata record.
- Every published dataset is accompanied by a **Data Quality Statement** describing the collection methodology, known limitations, quality control procedures, and completeness. This follows the **ISO 8000** data quality framework.
- The full processing history of each dataset is recorded in a lineage graph using **Apache Atlas** and the **Databricks Unity Catalog lineage feature**.
- Data-cleaning procedures, variable transformations, and analytical methods are documented.
- Research code is retained and published on GitHub under the MIT Licence.
- Where graziers consent to acknowledgement, their contribution is recognised in dataset citations using the **CRediT contributor taxonomy**. Graziers who prefer anonymity will not be named.
- Any future reuse of station data will follow the permissions established in this agreement.

### 7.5 Additional Open Science Commitments

- **Open code:** All analysis code, data pipelines, and ML model architectures are published under the MIT Licence on this GitHub repository.
- **Pre-registration:** Where feasible, research hypotheses and analytical plans are pre-registered on the **Open Science Framework (OSF)** before analysis begins.
- **Open peer review:** The Programme encourages submission to journals offering open peer review.
- **Data papers:** Significant new datasets are accompanied by a descriptive data paper (e.g., in *Scientific Data* or *Data in Brief*) so that grazier contributions receive formal academic citation credit.

## 9. Use of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

When appropriate, station data may be used to develop, train, validate, or evaluate statistical, machine-learning, or artificial-intelligence models.

Possible research applications may include:

- Prediction of livestock performance and live-weight gain
- Prediction of nutrient requirements and feed efficiency
- Prediction of animal health outcomes
- Pasture growth and feed-on-offer forecasting
- Stocking rate optimisation
- Prediction of environmental or greenhouse gas outcomes
- Development of farm decision-support tools for grazing management

Where possible, the research team will document:

- The variables used in the model
- Data-preprocessing procedures
- Training and validation methods
- Model performance metrics
- Important limitations and sources of uncertainty

AI or predictive model outputs will be treated as research results and should not automatically be considered professional veterinary, nutritional, financial, or management advice.

Where data is used for AI model training, this will be subject to specific additional consent (see Section 12). Graziers may opt out of AI training uses without affecting their participation in other aspects of the Programme.

## 14. Amendment Process

This Agreement is a living document. Changes to data uses, infrastructure, or grazier rights provisions require a structured amendment process.

### 14.1 Amendment Tiers

**Tier 1 — Material Amendment**

Applies to: changes to data use purposes, parties with access to data, categories of data collected, retention periods, licence terms, or grazier rights.

Requirements:
- Ethics Committee written approval
- Minimum **60 days** advance notice to all participating graziers
- Explicit re-consent required for continued participation
- Graziers who do not consent may withdraw without prejudice

**Tier 2 — Administrative Amendment**

Applies to: contact detail updates, cloud provider changes (where data protection standards are maintained), formatting updates, or clarificatory language that does not change the substance of grazier rights.

Requirements:
- Published on GitHub
- Minimum **30 days** email notice to all graziers
- No re-consent required; continued participation after notice period constitutes acceptance

### 14.2 Amendment Steps

1. **Internal Proposal & Classification** — The Research Team drafts the proposed amendment, classifies it as Tier 1 or Tier 2, and prepares a plain-English summary for grazier communication.
2. **DPO Review** — The Data Protection Officer reviews the proposal for compliance with applicable law, and may request modifications.
3. **Ethics Committee Approval (Tier 1 only)** — Typically takes 4–8 weeks.
4. **Grazier Notification** — All participating graziers are notified by email with the summary, a link to the full amended Agreement on GitHub, the tier classification, and the effective date.
5. **Consultation Period** — Graziers may submit questions or objections to the DPO during the notice period.
6. **Consent Collection or Continuation** — Tier 1 requires active re-signing; Tier 2 continuation constitutes acceptance.
7. **Publication on GitHub** — The new version is published with a commit message stating the version number, date, and description of changes. A `CHANGELOG.md` is maintained alongside this Agreement.

## 15. Withdrawal

The grazier may request that the research team stop collecting additional station data or stop using identifiable station data by contacting the research team.

The research team will make reasonable efforts to honour such requests.

However, it may not always be possible to remove data that have already been:

- Fully de-identified or anonymised
- Aggregated with other data
- Included in completed analyses
- Included in published research
- Incorporated into formally archived research records

The research team will explain any applicable limitations if a withdrawal request is made.

## 19. Grazier Rights

Under UK GDPR and this Agreement, every participating grazier has the following rights. The Research Institution will respond to any rights request within **30 calendar days** of receipt (extendable to 90 days for complex requests, with notification).

| Right | Basis | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Right of Access** | Article 15 UK GDPR | Request a copy of all personal data held, the purposes for which it is processed, who it has been shared with, and how long it will be retained |
| **Right to Rectification** | Article 16 | Request correction of inaccurate data or completion of incomplete data |
| **Right to Erasure** | Article 17 | Request deletion of personal data. Pseudonymised research data may be retained if required for scientific integrity under Article 17(3)(d), with explanation |
| **Right to Restriction** | Article 18 | Request that processing be paused while a dispute is resolved |
| **Right to Data Portability** | Article 20 | Receive all directly provided data in a structured, machine-readable format (CSV or JSON) |
| **Right to Object** | Article 21 | Object to processing based on legitimate interests at any time |
| **Right to Withdraw Consent** | UK GDPR | Withdraw consent to any or all processing at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of prior processing |
| **Right to Complain** | Article 77 | Lodge a complaint with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at [ico.org.uk](https://ico.org.uk) or call 0303 123 1113 |

To exercise any right: contact the DPO (Section 20), confirm your StationID and registered email address for identity verification, and allow 5 working days for acknowledgement and 30 calendar days for resolution.
